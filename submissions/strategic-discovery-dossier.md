# Strategic Discovery Dossier - Alex Tidey

## Legacy Trust Brief

### 1\. What problem is Legacy Trust trying to solve?

Legacy Trust is trying to improve the effectiveness and scalability of debt recovery by modernising their system, replacing a fragmented, manual operating model with a single, well understood framework supported by automation and self-service.

The main problems with the current system are as follows:

- Managers lose visibility of which follow-ups have happened.
- Statuses are updated inconsistently.
- Customers can experience slow and repetitive contact journeys.
- Agents spend significant time checking whether customers have already been contacted.
- Follow-ups and promises to pay are manually tracked.
- Information is reconciled across spreadsheets, emails and the legacy database.
- Straightforward cases and complex cases are handled through the same manual flow.

### 2\. Why is the current way of working failing at scale?

The current process relies on a 20-year-old collections database, spreadsheets, emails and local workarounds. These may have worked at lower volumes, but with 100,000+ delinquent accounts and 50+ agents, they create significant manual overhead. Agents spend time reconciling information, checking previous contact, tracking follow-ups and updating statuses. This leads to duplicate work, missed actions, inconsistent data and poor management visibility.

The core issue is that the official process does not fully reflect the work happening on the ground. As volumes grow, this dependence on manual effort and individual memory becomes increasingly difficult to manage and limits the team's capacity to focus on complex cases requiring human judgement.

### 3\. What evidence would leadership expect before approving Phase 1?

The following groups would expect the following evidence:

#### Debt Recovery Operations

- Evidence where current processes create missed follow ups, duplicate effort, and wasted handling time.
- A clear map of where work is currently breaking down.
- Proof of which automation ideas will save the most time.
- A realistic future-state process that her team can actually follow.

#### Finance and Compliance

- Transparent assumptions.
- Sensible sensitivity testing.
- Value case separating hard savings from hoped-for revenue uplift.

#### Collections Team

- A clear and honest As-Is process.
- Future-state workflow that shows where agent work begins and ends.

#### Product Management

- Traceable analysis, showing how pain points in the current process link to opportunities, future-state workflows, and eventually buildable requirements.

### Customer evidence limitation

The current case materials do not provide direct customer research, feedback or behavioural data. Therefore, customer needs and adoption risks should be treated as hypotheses to validate rather than established facts. Where customer evidence is unavailable, the discovery should clearly record the assumption and identify it as a gap before Phase 1 decisions are made.

### Opportunity prioritisation

Automation opportunities should be prioritised considering the following:

- 12-month ROI
- P&L impact
- Implementation effort
- Compliance and operational risk
- Customer impact/adoption
- Technical dependencies and complexity

This will help ensure that the highest-ranked opportunity is not simply the one with the largest theoretical return, but the one offering the strongest credible and deliverable value for Phase 1.

## Final Problem Statement

Legacy Trust's debt recovery operation is struggling to scale because agents rely on a fragmented combination of a 20-year-old collections database, spreadsheets, emails and manual workarounds to manage 100,000+ delinquent accounts. This creates duplicated effort, missed follow-ups, inconsistent status information and slow customer journeys, reducing agent capacity and contributing to an estimated 15% revenue loss.

Legacy Trust needs to identify which parts of the recovery process can be safely and effectively automated or moved to self-service, while preserving human judgement for complex cases, and determine whether a focused Phase 1 can deliver credible operational and financial value within 12 months.
